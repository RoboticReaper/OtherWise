## Follow-up priorities

1. **Strengthen graph evidence.** Of 164 relations, four are source-asserted with citations; 160 are explicitly editorial and have no source IDs. Of those editorial edges, 106 use one of three generic boilerplate explanations; 54 have topic-specific rationales. Editorial connections are allowed by the agreed prompt, so this is not a card-admission failure. However, generic notes do not explain why a particular connection is useful. Replace them with concept-specific rationales and citations where a relationship is asserted as fact. Preserve the editorial/source-asserted distinction when weighting graph signals.
2. **Review equivalences before merging.** The file correctly defers three overlaps: authored Urban heat islands versus `Q215712`, Silk Road exchanges versus `Q36288`, and authored Confirmation bias versus `Q431498`. Establish whether each pair is equivalent or has a distinct learning takeaway; do not merge by label alone. This prevents duplicate suggestions without losing a legitimate narrower facet.
3. **Test additional concept discovery before bulk expansion.** Most cards research identities already present: 177 of 200. The three new local concepts are Python list comprehensions, springback, and overbending; the latter two were examples in the prompt. This is a useful identity/card workflow test, but limited evidence for extracting many previously uncataloged details. A next validation batch should deliberately discover fresh source-backed ideas and applications beyond those examples, with measured novelty, duplicate rate, and source support.

Many cards use similar exploration phrasing (95 contain “exploring,” 55 contain “comparing,” and 72 contain “reveals”; these counts overlap). This is an optional writing refinement, not a demonstrated factual or admission defect. Most explanations give a concrete learning angle. Actual curiosity and retained interest still require evaluation beyond this catalog audit.
