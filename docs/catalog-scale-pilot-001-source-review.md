# Catalog scale pilot 001: fresh source review

Reviewed on 2026-10-08. This review checked 20 delivered cards, four from each of batches 003–007, against live official, first-party and authoritative educational sources. Nineteen cards had no substantive finding. **Chemigram needs one qualification correction:** its card, takeaway and evidence note make resists part of the definition, although the inspected sources allow chemigrams without them. One further finding concerns the precision of card-level provenance for Feature (archaeology). All 21 attached relationships had an adequate factual basis; Chemigram's editorial comparison should adopt the same qualification correction.

This is a purposeful fresh sample across domains, named subjects, ambiguous words, `source_asserted` relationships and scientific/legal qualifications. All 20 IDs are outside their batch's original 40-card `sample_ids`; membership was checked against the delivered frozen samples. It is **not an unbiased estimate of defects in the 1,000-card pilot**. The review assessed the delivered content against the frozen baseline and inspected relation targets in that baseline or the delivered batches. Current-project identity reconciliation is a separate integration check.

## Findings and proposed correction

**R1 — substantive qualification: Chemigram (`local:catalog:chemigram`, batch 006).** The delivered first sentence and takeaway present resists as necessary; its evidence note also attributes resists to the V&A passage without preserving the passage's qualification. The [V&A's Chemigram entry](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/photographic-processes), opening paragraph, says oils or varnishes are used *often*. More decisively, [Pierre Cordier's English process diagram](https://pierrecordier.com/15.html), the two branches immediately above “Definition,” includes chemigrams both with and without a localizing product, explicitly equating that product with a resist. His [lexicon](https://www.pierrecordier.com/18.html), “localizing product,” confirms the term and examples. This is a scope error in the definition, not a reason to reject the subject or its photogram comparison.

Proposed replacement card, **39 words**:

> A chemigram creates an image by manipulating photographic paper with chemicals, often using oils or varnishes. Working in full light combines the maker’s intervention with chance. The marks emerge from material reactions without a camera recording an external scene.

Proposed takeaway:

> Chemigrams use photographic chemistry in full light without a camera; optional oils or varnishes and experimental intervention can shape the marks.

Proposed evidence note:

> The V&A describes direct manipulation of photographic paper, often using oils or varnishes, in full light with chance. Cordier’s diagram includes chemigrams with and without a localizing product; his definition confirms the camera-free process.

Retain `related_to` / `editorial` / `local:catalog:photogram`, with this note:

> Compare direct chemical manipulation of photographic paper, sometimes shaped by oils or varnishes, with photograms made by exposing paper beneath objects to light.

The V&A's “Chemigram” and “Photogram” entries support the revised comparison. Cordier's diagram, lexicon and [one-page definition](https://www.pierrecordier.com/IMG/pdf/definition_EN.pdf), final definition paragraph, support the optional materials and camera-free process. Keep `a04` for the comparison; add Cordier's diagram as card evidence for the qualification correction. These are proposed reviewed changes; this review did not alter any delivered candidate.

**Correction recheck:** the 39-word card, revised takeaway and relationship note in [card-corrections.json](/Users/baorenliu/Documents/Programming/Python/ProductSpace/data/catalog-candidates/scale-pilot-001/local-review/card-corrections.json) match the wording above. Its version-2 proposal retains the identity and scope, qualifies optional materials, and adds the inventor's diagram/definition as evidence. Those passages were read directly in this review. The overlay is recorded as `source_verified_correction_not_applied`; the table below continues to assess the original delivery.

**R2 — minor provenance improvement: Feature (archaeology) (`Q814254`, batch 006).** `h04` directly supports the nonportable definition and examples. The card's recording/context explanation is a reasonable inference when read with `h02`, but only `h04` appears in its card-level evidence. Add `h02` with locator “Context discussion; geographic context, associations and documenting context” and a note that the explanation connects nonportability with documenting location and associations. `h02` already exists in this candidate's sources and editorial relationship, so no new factual claim or identity is needed. The [NPS context article](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/what-is-archeological-context.htm), opening discussion, supports this addition.

## Coverage and access limits

All **25 unique URLs cited for the sampled cards and their relations** yielded relevant passages in this review. Supporting passages were read, not inferred from the existence of a URL or an authored audit/access log. Additional corroboration consisted of the FDA pulse-oximeter page, MIT's Borel–Cantelli lecture, Cordier's French/English process diagrams, English definition and lexicon, and Frey/Jegen's publisher abstract. The [French diagram](https://www.pierrecordier.com/lechimigramme.html), with/without branches and definition, agreed with the inspected English version.

For the four sampled cards with Wikidata identity URLs, live records were inspected for centimorgan, Herschel and archaeological feature. [Q6917879](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q6917879) failed to fetch twice; this is an unresolved remote identity check, not evidence that the identifier is wrong. A Cordier legacy URL (`/5.html`) also failed, but the English diagram and lexicon subsequently supplied the required primary evidence. Frey's [author-hosted full article](https://www.bsfrey.ch/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/motivation-crowding-theory.pdf) returned garbled extracted text and its screenshot was unavailable; only the legible publisher abstract was used. Search snippets and failed pages were not counted as inspected support.

This review supports correcting R1 and tightening R2 before promoting the sampled content. It does not establish that every pilot card is correct, that the original authors actually browsed each source, that shared sources have no other dependencies, or that the frozen baseline is reconciled with the current project catalog. It also supplies no evidence that a recommender built from these cards produces sustained interest expansion or broader retained interests. That requires separate evaluation.
